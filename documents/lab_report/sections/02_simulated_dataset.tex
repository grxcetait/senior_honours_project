% [1] https://meer21cm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/
A cosmological neutral hydrogen (HI) intensity mapping simulation was generated using the meer21cm package (\cite{Meer21cmMeer21cmDocumentation}), which allows for the independent generation of the various signal components expected in a real HI intensity mapping observation, such as the 21\,cm signal, smooth foreground emission, and instrumental noise.

% [1] https://inspirehep.net/literature/3094605#:~:text=Citations%20per%20year&text=The%20MeerKAT%20Large%20Area%20Synoptic,5%E2%80%B2%E2%80%B2.
% [2] https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/469/1/787/3098176
The mock 21\,cm signal was generated adopting the MeerKAT Large Area Synoptic Survey (MeerKLASS) survey observed with the MeerKAT radio telescope in the L-band data (\cite{manglaMeerKLASSLbandOntheFly2025}). This corresponds to a redshift range of approximately z = 0.39 - 0.46, targeting the post-reionisation epoch. While the L-band covers a range of 856 - 1712\,MHz, this dataset will cover a smaller frequency range of 971.2 - 1023.6\,MHz, allowing for a more focused analysis. Over the narrow frequency range studied here, the redshift evolution of the HI signal is negligible and is not modelled.

% [1] https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.473.1879R/abstract#:~:text=Le%20F%C3%A8vre%2C%20Olivier-,Abstract,10.48550/arXiv.1709.07596
A flat-sky approximation was adopted since, assuming that the sky area is small enough that a Cartesian grid projection can be used. The HI abundance parameter, which defines the ratio of the neutral hydrogen density to the critical density of the Universe at a given redshift, was set to $\Omega_{HI} = 5 \times 10^{-4}$, consistent with observational constraints at this redshift (\cite{rheeNeutralHydrogenGas2018b}).

% [2] https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2011/01/aa13628-09/aa13628-09.html
The foreground signal models Galactic synchrotron emission by extrapolating an 408\,MHz map to the survey frequency range using a power-law spectral index of $\alpha = -2.7$, consistent with the expected spectral behaviour of synchrotron radiation (\cite{guzmanAllskyGalacticRadiation2011a}). This simulation was then projected onto the same sky pixelisation as the mock HI signal. 

%Maps generated with just the 21\,cm signal and the 21\,cm signal with foreground can be seen in \Cref{fig:map_w_wo_foreground}. The colour scales for both distributions are different, with the foreground being four orders of magnitude larger than the 21\,cm signal. 

By inputting the cosmological models and survey parameters, a mock 21\,cm intensity mapping signal can be simulated. This map is mean-centred across the different frequency channels. This means the mean-centred total map is the sum of the mean-centred components. The mock data uses a fixed seed number to generate the random fields, giving one realisation. This can be projected onto the sky where other signals, such as noise and foregrounds, can be added. However, instrumental noise and other signals found in real observations were not included in order to isolate the performance of the foreground removal methods. It is important to note that this simulation represents an idealised portrayal of expected observational data. 

The normalised frequency covariance matrices for the total signal, foreground, and HI signal components are shown in \Cref{fig:tot_fg_hi_noise_covariance}. The foreground covariance is nearly uniform across all frequency pairs, where emission at one frequency is strongly correlated with emission at all other frequencies. This reflects its highly smooth spectral behaviour. In contrast, the HI signal covariance is sharply concentrated along the diagonal. 

\Cref{fig:frequency_vs_temperature} further highlights the difference in the frequency structures of the HI signal and foregrounds, where the foregrounds are smooth while the HI signal varies rapidly across frequencies. It is clear that the foreground dominates the total signal, being four orders of magnitude larger than the HI signal. 

This fundamental difference in covariance structure, combined with the foreground being approximately four orders of magnitude brighter than the HI signal, is what both PCA and GPR exploit to achieve foreground separation.
