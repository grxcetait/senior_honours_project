\begin{abstract}
The LHCb experiment at CERN investigates charge-parity (CP) violation to better understand the observed matter-antimatter asymmetry in the Universe. Its Ring Imaging Cherenkov (RICH) detectors identify charged particles using Cherenkov radiation, but the higher luminosity planned for Upgrade II requires new detector configurations which can maintain performance under the harsh conditions. Two critical benchmarks are defined: an overall Cherenkov angle resolution better than 0.5\,mrad and an occupancy below 30\,\%. This study simulated 24 detector configurations, investigating contributions of emission point, chromatic dispersion, and pixel size on the resolution. Six configurations achieved the resolution benchmark, and two were selected for detail occupancy studies. Of these, only the configuration using an LAPPD photodetector with a 250\,nm wavelength cut-off and 1.4\,mm pixel size satisfied both benchmarks. 
\end{abstract}
